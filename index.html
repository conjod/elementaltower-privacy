Privacy Policy

Elemental Tower Survivor · com.cjgames.elementaltowersurvivor

Last updated: 29 October 2026

This policy explains what Elemental Tower Survivor collects, why, and how to have it deleted. It covers the Android app and the browser version of the game.

The short version. The game has no sign-in screen. It never asks for your name, email address, phone number or location, and it never collects them. What it does send is gameplay data — how far you got, which abilities you bought, how smoothly the game ran — filed under a nickname the game invents for you and a random anonymous ID, not under anything that identifies you.

The Android app has one advert, and it is optional: when your tower falls, you can watch a short video to revive. The adverts come from Google AdMob, which collects some information about your device to show them (section 7). In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent first, and you can refuse.

Who is responsible

The game is made and published by CJ Games, an individual developer. Contact details are at the bottom of this page.

What the game sends, and why

1. Your in-game nickname

The first time you play, the game generates a nickname for you (for example “BraveBear65”) and stores it on your device. You do not type it and are never asked to. You can reroll it for a different random one. This nickname is attached to your leaderboard entries, run records and feedback so that they can be told apart from everyone else's.

Because it is invented by the game rather than supplied by you, the nickname does not identify you to us or to anyone else — unless you choose to tell people what yours is.

2. An anonymous player ID

The first time the game goes online, it creates an anonymous account for your copy of the game with Google Firebase Authentication. This happens automatically: there is no sign-in screen, and no name, email address, password or Google account is involved. The account is just a random ID (a string of letters and numbers), and the key that proves it is yours is stored on your device.

The ID is attached to your leaderboard entries, run records, error reports and feedback. It is used for two things: so that only your copy of the game can change your leaderboard entry, and to limit how often a copy of the game can send data, which stops spam. It is not linked to anything that identifies you. If you uninstall the game or clear its data, the key is lost and a new ID is created the next time you play.

3. Leaderboard entries

When you set a new personal best, the game submits one entry to the global leaderboard so the board can be shown to all players. The entry contains: your nickname, your anonymous player ID, how long you survived, the wave you reached, how many shop rerolls and revives you used, which tower you played, the date, and a label saying which platform you played on (Android, web, and so on).

Runs with and without a revive are ranked on separate boards. Only your best run on each board is stored; a new best overwrites the previous one.

4. Run records

At the end of every run — and as a snapshot if you close the game mid-run — the game submits a record of that run. If the device is offline, the record is kept on the device and sent the next time the game goes online. This is used to balance the game and to find performance problems on hardware the developer does not own.

The record contains gameplay and performance information: your nickname, your anonymous player ID, the build of the game you played, the tower you chose, the wave and time you reached, kills, which abilities you bought and when, damage and healing per ability, which boss relics you took, how the run ended, whether you watched the revive advert (watched, closed early, none available, or failed to load, with the error code), whether the genuine-app check in section 8 succeeded (or the error it returned), which towers you have unlocked, how many runs you have finished, a summary of frame times and of how much memory the game used during the run, and the same platform label as above.

It also contains a few general characteristics of your device, so that slow runs can be grouped by the kind of hardware they happened on: the number of processor cores, the approximate amount of memory, the screen size and pixel density, and which graphics mode the game ran in. It does not include the device's make, model, or any serial number or other hardware identifier.

5. Error reports

If the game hits a fault while you are playing — a bug that makes it freeze or stop responding — it sends a short report so the fault can be found and fixed. Nothing is sent unless something actually goes wrong, and each distinct fault is reported once.

The report contains the technical error message and the location in the game’s code that produced it, the build of the game you were playing, the platform label, your screen resolution, your device’s browser/OS identification string, your anonymous player ID, and — so the fault can be reproduced — the wave, time and tower of the run you were on. It contains nothing you have typed and nothing about you.

6. Feedback you send

The game has a feedback form. It is entirely optional and nothing is sent unless you write something and tap Send. When you do, the game submits the text you wrote (up to 1,000 characters), your nickname, your anonymous player ID, the date and time, the platform label, your screen resolution, and your device's browser/OS identification string, together with the wave, time and tower of the run you were on. The last three are included so a bug report can be reproduced on the right kind of device.

Please do not put personal information — your real name, email address, or anything else identifying — into the feedback box. If you do, it will be stored along with the rest of your message.

Adverts and security checks by Google (Android app only)

The browser version of the game has no adverts and makes neither of these checks.

7. The optional revive advert (Google AdMob)

When your tower falls, the Android app offers one revive per run. To take it, you watch a short video advert. Nothing else in the game shows adverts, and you never have to watch one: declining the revive simply ends the run.

The adverts are provided by Google AdMob. So that an advert is ready when you need it, the app starts loading one before each run, which means Google's advertising software contacts Google even in runs where you never watch one. Google's software collects:

  • your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location;
  • your interactions with adverts, such as whether one was shown, watched or tapped;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hangs and energy use;
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.

Google uses this to show adverts, measure them and prevent fraud. It does so as an independent controller, under its own privacy policy: see how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. The developer receives only overall figures from AdMob, such as how many adverts were shown, and never information about you.

Your choices. In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, the first time you open the app Google's consent form asks whether your data may be used to personalise adverts. You can agree, choose what to allow, or refuse. If you refuse, the revive advert still works, but it is not personalised. You can change your answer at any time from the game's Options menu (Privacy). Wherever you are, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (on most phones: Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads).

Adverts are limited to content rated suitable for teenagers (Google's “T” rating or below).

8. The genuine-app check (Firebase App Check)

To protect the leaderboard and game data from fake submissions, the Android app asks Google Play to confirm that it is a genuine copy of the game, installed from Google Play, on a genuine device. This uses Google's Play Integrity service, through Firebase App Check. Google Play checks the app's name, version and signature and the integrity of the device, and returns a short-lived token that the game attaches to its connections to Firebase. The token says only that the check passed. It does not identify you or your device, and App Check does not keep the information used to make it.

What the game does not collect

  • No sign-in, username or password. The anonymous player ID above is created automatically and holds nothing about you.
  • No real name, email address, phone number, or postal address.
  • No location or GPS data.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone.
  • The game itself never reads or stores your advertising ID. Only Google's advertising software in the Android app uses it, as described in section 7.
  • No analytics SDK. The game does not track you across other apps or websites. Google's advertising software may use your advertising ID to personalise adverts, which you can refuse or turn off (section 7).
  • No payment information. The game is free and has no in-app purchases.

Data stored only on your device

Your save data — unlocked towers, your nickname, settings, tutorial progress and your local best score — is kept in your device's local storage and is not uploaded, except where described above. The key for your anonymous player ID is kept there too, as are the records of any runs that could not be sent because the device was offline, until the game next goes online. Uninstalling the app, or clearing the site data in your browser, erases all of it permanently. There is no cloud save and no way to recover it.

Where the data goes

The leaderboard entries, run records, error reports and feedback described above are stored in a Google Firebase Realtime Database hosted in the europe-west1 region (Belgium). The anonymous player ID is managed by Google Firebase Authentication. Google acts as the data processor for both and handles the data under Google's Firebase privacy terms. This data is not sold, and is not shared with anyone else. (What Google's advertising software collects in the Android app goes directly to Google; see section 7.)

Like any online service, Google's servers see your device's IP address and browser/OS identification string when the game connects to them. Under Google's terms, Firebase keeps these in its own logs for a limited time (a few days for the database, a few weeks for Authentication, where they are used to protect against abuse). The game itself does not store your IP address.

The leaderboard is public within the game: any player can see the nicknames, times and waves on it. Run records, error reports and feedback are not shown to other players.

How long it is kept

Leaderboard entries are kept for as long as the leaderboard exists. Run records, error reports and feedback are kept while they are useful for balancing and fixing the game. There is no fixed deletion schedule, and anything you ask to have removed is removed.

Deleting your data

Email the address below and ask, and your leaderboard entries, run records, error reports, feedback and anonymous player ID will be deleted.

Because the game holds nothing that identifies you, the only way to find your data is by your in-game nickname — so please include it. You can see your current nickname on the game's home screen. If you have rerolled it, mention the previous ones you remember.

You can also stop any further data being sent at any time by uninstalling the game. Playing offline only delays it: the records of runs played offline are sent the next time the game goes online.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and does not knowingly collect information from them. Its only advert is the optional revive advert, limited to content suitable for teenagers, and it offers no purchases. If you believe a child has sent feedback containing personal information, contact the address below and it will be deleted.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts collecting something new, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top of this page will change.
